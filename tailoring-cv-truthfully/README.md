# Tailoring a CV truthfully

A tailored CV has two readers: the applicant tracking system (ATS) that parses and ranks it, then a person who skims it for about ten seconds. This skill wins both without inventing anything. Speed comes from a fixed process; trust comes from rules that apply from the first draft, not at the final check.

## Scope and data handling

- This skill helps a candidate present their own experience. It does not screen, rank or assess candidates for an employer: that use is high-risk under the EU AI Act and is out of scope.
- Use only material the candidate provides in the conversation: their CV, an experience summary, or answers to questions.
- Never write government ID numbers, bank details, date of birth, photo or nationality into the CV.
- Ask before naming a client, product or project the candidate may be bound to keep confidential (NDA, unreleased product, patent pending). If unsure, describe it in general terms.

## Step 1: Gather inputs

- The job description (JD): pasted text, a file or a link. If a link can't be read, ask for the text.
- The candidate's experience: latest CV plus any fuller notes they have. Newer facts from the conversation override older documents.
- Target country (sets spelling and page size: A4 or US Letter).

## Step 2: Extract requirements

List the JD's requirements in its exact phrasing, adding the abbreviation or full form ("large language models (LLMs)").
- Tier 1: must-haves (listed as essential, repeated, or in the title)
- Tier 2: nice-to-haves
- Tier 3: culture and values signals

## Step 3: Build a requirement-to-evidence matrix

Before writing any bullet, map each requirement to the 1-2 strongest pieces of evidence across all roles. Choose by: closest match > clearest result > recency > seniority signal. Always add one row: "Ownership and seniority" (what they led, designed or owned end to end; people led or mentored; senior audiences; proposals won).

Mark each row:
- Strong: direct, recent evidence with a result
- Weak: indirect or partial evidence
- Missing: no evidence yet

## Step 4: Check the gaps before drafting

A missing row may be something the candidate did but never wrote down. Ask before treating it as a gap.
- Send all questions in one message. Cover every Tier 1 row marked weak or missing.
- For each: 1-2 short, concrete questions naming the requirement in plain words and where it might have happened. Ask for the facts a bullet needs: what they did, their role (led / contributed / supported), scale, outcome.
- Never suggest an answer. "No" is a fine answer.
- If the candidate isn't available, draft with gaps marked and put the questions at the top of the output.

## Truth rules (non-negotiable)

1. Every line traces to something the candidate provided. Keep the source for each bullet.
2. Allowed: reorder, cut, merge, generalise, surface buried results, and use the JD's term when it accurately describes what they did ("user interviews" → "user research": fine; "survey analysis" → "SQL": not fine).
3. Not allowed: new or rounded-up numbers, tools or methods they didn't use, changed job titles, implied ownership they didn't have.
4. Verbs match real ownership in both directions. Inflating is wrong ("contributed" → "led"); so is deflating ("led" → "helped"). Accuracy, not modesty.
5. If a stronger claim might be true, write the version the evidence supports and add [CONFIRM: ...].
6. A requirement is a true gap only after the candidate says no. True gaps go to the cover letter or interview prep, never into the CV by stretching.

## Writing the bullets

**Shape:** result or consequence + what they did + how (keyword in context). Outcome first when there is one.
- Weak: "Ran usability tests for a health app"
- Stronger: "Found and fixed the main usability problems in a health app over two testing rounds, including a design change that won users' trust"

**Results count without numbers:** adoption ("adopted by the wider team"), decisions informed ("set the launch price"), scale (sessions, countries, budget), efficiency (hours freed), recognition (promotion). Only numbers the candidate gave; otherwise action-only plus [CONFIRM: outcome?].

**Clarity:** each bullet must make sense to a stranger. Name the product type and the user in a few words; translate internal project names and acronyms. One idea per bullet, 1-2 lines.

**Detail:** keep a detail only if it matches a JD keyword, signals scale or stakes, or makes a result credible. At most 1-2 numbers per bullet.

**Verbs:** use the most precise verb the evidence supports. No opening verb more than twice in the CV, never twice in one role. Each main role shows what they owned before the tasks.

**Human voice:** no "spearheaded, leveraged, orchestrated, championed, pioneered, results-driven, passionate, synergy, robust, seamless, cutting-edge". No bold or labels at the start of bullets.

## Layout (ATS-safe)

- Single column. No tables, text boxes, icons, images, headers or footers.
- Sections: SUMMARY, WORK EXPERIENCE, EDUCATION, SKILLS (languages as the last line of Skills).
- Top: name, a one-line headline of the target role area plus 2-3 focus areas (no title they haven't held), contact line.
- Each role: "Company — Job Title, City" with dates right-aligned; optional one-line company descriptor.
- Max 2 pages. Reverse chronological. Uneven depth is fine: 3-7 bullets for the most relevant roles, 1-3 for older ones.

## Keyword placement

- Use the JD's exact phrase at least once. Tier 1 keywords: 2-3 placements (headline or summary, a results bullet, Skills). Tier 2: once.
- Every Skills item must be proven by a bullet above it. No keyword dumps; no hidden or white text.

## Final check

For every bullet:
1. Would a hiring manager understand it without knowing the candidate or the company?
2. Does every detail earn its place?
3. Does it say what changed because of their work?
4. Is it true and sourced?
5. Is the verb accurate, neither inflated nor deflated?

Then render the file and check it visually: dates aligned, nothing orphaned, within 2 pages.

## Output

1. The tailored CV (.docx and PDF).
2. Requirement-to-evidence matrix: requirement | evidence | source | where it appears.
3. Gap check: questions asked | answers | what changed. True gaps listed with one idea each for the cover letter or interview.
4. Change log: change | why (JD keyword or value) | source. Include what was cut, so the candidate can put it back.
5. ATS check: Tier 1 coverage before → after.
6. [CONFIRM] list, asked before finalising if any item affects the headline, summary or top bullets.
